Places where you're not obligated to be.

The sociologist [Ray Oldenburg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ray_Oldenburg) defined it in 1989: your home is the first place, your work the second. [The third](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Third_place) is where you relax without socioeconomic status mattering. A park bench. A public library.

**They aren't digital spaces.** A Discord server or a Facebook group doesn't count. They can help organize in-person meetups, but the real third place is physical.

Meanwhile, work keeps trying to be your second home. They put in "nice" cafés, couches, games. But it isn't spontaneous — it's a trap disguised as a perk.

**The difference is simple:** in a third place, nobody is watching you. Nobody expects you to produce. You can be alone or with others, with no obligation.

And you can't manufacture them. A third place **is born from the community**, not from someone building one and calling it that. A card shop doesn't succeed because someone opened it — it succeeds because there were people playing who wanted a place.

What if, instead of designing offices that pretend to be living spaces, we created more real living spaces?

Maybe we already have them — we just haven't observed them closely enough to see a seed in what is currently only three people running in the park.
